Burberry — Trench Coat Navigation Redesign

v1.0 · July 2026
Prepared by: Mahalakshmi

A concept redesign of us.burberry.com, focused specifically on the trench coat discovery experience — treated as a real PM exercise: problem framing, users, requirements, and scope.

Self-Directed PRD Comparative Audit Concept Redesign

1. Background

Burberry's website carries genuine 170-year heritage — the trench coat, the check pattern, real craft history. But the current site fragments its most iconic product across two separate nav paths, and buries the heritage story that should set it apart from every other luxury site.

2. Problem Statement

Burberry's trench coat is reachable through two inconsistent nav paths: one under Women → Coats & Jackets → Trench Coats with no style breakdown, and a second, standalone "Trench" nav item that reveals 6 distinct styles (Mayfair, Kensington, Waterloo, Chelsea, Fitzrovia, Camden) — but by name only, with no visual way to compare them. Shoppers following the first path may never discover the styles exist at all; those who find them still can't tell what's different without clicking into each one.

3. Users & Personas

"Heritage Hannah"
Knows the brand's history

Wants to feel that heritage while shopping. Frustrated when craft content feels disconnected from the shopping flow, and wants to understand why a style looks the way it does — not just its name.

"First-Time Felix"
Comparing against other luxury sites

Needs to quickly tell styles apart without deep-diving into each product page — will bounce to a competitor if comparison is too slow.

5. Current-State Audit

  • Two competing nav paths lead to the same product family, with inconsistent depth.
  • Style names alone don't differentiate — Kensington vs. Chelsea vs. Camden give no visual or descriptive cue.
  • Heritage content is siloed on a separate page, disconnected from the shopping flow.
  • No side-by-side comparison view exists anywhere on the site.

This finding is part of a broader pattern — see the Comparative Analysis for how Chanel, Fendi, Balmain, and Balenciaga show similar structural sameness. Burberry was selected as the deep-dive subject because its nav-fragmentation issue was the most specific and fixable finding.

6. Scope

In scope
  • Homepage hero (heritage story surfaced)
  • Unified Trench Coat page with visual style comparison
  • One product page (single style, size/color selection)
Out of scope (this pass)
  • Full site rebuild or other product categories
  • Cart, checkout, payment integration
  • Men's and Children's trench experiences

8. Redesigned Information Architecture

Home → Trench Coats → Product Page. One unified path, replacing the current split between "Coats & Jackets → Trench Coats" and a standalone "Trench" nav item.
